# 08 — Adversarial verification run (deep-research workflow)

Run completed 2026-09-26. The workflow searched five angles in parallel, fetched the top 15 sources, and extracted checkable claims. Three independent verifiers then voted on each claim, and a claim needed two refute votes to be dropped. It used 107 agents and 917 tool calls.

**How it was used in the report:** it confirmed the report's main defect and recall findings, and it added Ford TSB 23-2123 and the 2017–2019 Highlander V6. It voted down the 2025 iSeeCars figures only because they are out of date, not because they were wrong. See the report's method section.

## Summary

For a US buyer looking at 2015–2025 gas vehicles, the strongest picks are mostly Toyota and Lexus models from the last year before a redesign, with proven naturally aspirated engines. Sedans: the 2023 Lexus IS, 2023 Camry and 2023 Corolla, all 2026 J.D. Power VDS winners. SUVs: the 2023 Lexus GX 460 (4.6L V8), the 2023 4Runner (4.0L V6) and the 2017–2019 Highlander with the 3.5L V6 and 8-speed, plus the pre-2023 Sequoia on iSeeCars longevity data. Pickups: the 2023 Tacoma and the 2020+ Honda Ridgeline 3.5L V6. Consumer Reports' value picks add the 2016 Mazda6 four-cylinder and the 2020 Honda Fit, and GM's 2023 Equinox, Tahoe, Enclave and XT6 won VDS segment awards. The clearest engine to avoid is Toyota's 3.4L V35A twin-turbo V6 in the 2022–2024 Tundra, 2022–2024 Lexus LX and 2024 Lexus GX. Three rolling recalls (24V381, 25V767, 26V320) cover roughly 270,000 of these for debris damage to the main bearings. As of September 2026, the 25V767 remedy is a bearing inspection, and the engine is replaced only if the inspection fails. Other avoids backed by manufacturer documents: 2017–2018 Altima and 2018–2019 Sentra/Versa CVTs (their warranty extension has now mostly expired); 2017–2020 3.5L EcoBoost F-150s and 2018–2020 Expedition/Navigators without a record of cam phaser replacement (Ford's program expired); 10R80 transmissions with a history of harsh shifting; and Hyundai/Kia Nu, Gamma and Theta II MPI engines unless they are still inside the 15-year/150,000-mile settlement warranty. Consumer Reports' 2026 worst-used list (entries confirmed through secondary coverage) adds the 2020 Explorer, 2021 Tahoe/Suburban/Yukon, 2016/2019/2021/2022 Wrangler, 2025 Ram 1500 and 2025 Colorado/Canyon. In contrast, 2019–2020 Subarus with CVTs now carry a 10-year/100,000-mile CVT extension, which makes them lower-risk buys under 100,000 miles. Three issues in the brief — the GM 6.2L L87 recall, Honda 1.5T oil dilution and GM AFM/DFM lifter failures — had no claims survive verification, so they still need a check by VIN.

### Toyota's V35A twin-turbo V6 has a manufacturing defect: machining debris left in the engine can stick to the crankshaft main bearings and cause them to fail under sustained high load. The symptoms are knocking, rough running, a no-start, or a stall with loss of motive power. Recall 25V767 (filed Nov 6, 2025) covers 126,691 US vehicles: 2022–2024 Tundra (113,079, built Nov 22, 2021 to Feb 14, 2024), 2022–2024 Lexus LX (9,895) and 2024 Lexus GX (3,717). It extends the earlier recall 24V381, which covered engines built before that window.

- Confidence: high · Vote: 3-0 ([8],[9],[10] merged)
- Sources: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCLRPT-25V767-6304.pdf, NHTSA acknowledgment RCAK-25V767-5381

Toyota's Part 573 report says: 'engine machining debris of a particular size and amount may not have been cleared from the engine during manufacturing ... failure of the bearings may occur ... engine knocking, engine rough running, engine no start and/or an engine stall.' It also says: 'The same engines ... produced prior to this period were recalled under 24V 381. Some of the same engines produced after this period were produced with improved manufacturing processes and remain under investigation. Others were produced with an improved main bearing.' At filing, the recall carried a Do Not Drive / Park Outside advisory, and no remedy existed yet. Toyota's newsroom, CBS News, Fox Business and a June 2026 teardown write-up on PickupTruckTalk (swarf in the oil passages, spun main bearings) agree.

### Status as of September 2026: the V35A investigation that was still open under 25V767 led to a third recall, 26V320 (May 20, 2026), adding 43,566 2024 Tundras built Feb 7 to Aug 5, 2024 with engines from Toyota's Alabama plant. The three recalls together cover about 270,000 vehicles. Engines with the improved main bearing (roughly July 2024 production onward) are excluded, though Toyota says it is still monitoring them. The 25V767 remedy was amended in June 2026 to a software- and drive-data-based inspection of the #1 main bearing, with the engine replaced only if the inspection fails. Owners under 24V381 still get a guaranteed engine replacement.

- Confidence: medium · Vote: Derived from verifier evidence on 3-0 claims [8],[10]
- Sources: NHTSA recall report RCLRPT-26V320-0076, Amended 25V767 report RCLRPT-25V767-3307 (June 2026, hosted by thedrive.com), https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2025/RCLRPT-25V767-6304.pdf

The 26V320 chronology says bearings from the period under investigation 'contained the same wear pattern as those covered by 24V-381 and 25V-767'. The amended 25V767 filing changes only the remedy, not the vehicle population. One secondary source (carsmultiverse, checked Sep 9, 2026) says owner letters went out Aug 28, 2026. Confidence is medium because the remedy split (inspection vs. guaranteed replacement) and the July 2024 improved-bearing cutoff come partly from secondary sources. Practical advice: avoid 2022 through mid-2024 V35A trucks unless a VIN check shows the recall work, or a replaced engine, is documented.

### Hyundai/Kia's second engine settlement (Engine II, final approval April 9, 2024) covers about 1.05M Hyundai and 1.16M Kia vehicles with the 2.4L Theta II MPI, 2.0L Nu GDI and 1.6L Gamma GDI engines. Gas models in scope include the 2014–2016 Elantra, 2014–2020 Elantra GT, 2010–2021 Tucson, 2012–2017 Veloster, 2010–2018 Forte and 2012–2019 Soul. It gives a transferable 15-year/150,000-mile extended warranty for connecting-rod-bearing damage. The earlier 2019 Theta II GDI settlement gave a lifetime short-block warranty instead.

- Confidence: high · Vote: 3-0 ([11],[12] merged)
- Sources: https://www.hyundainews.com/releases/3657, Kia press release 19400 (kiamedia.com), In re Hyundai and Kia Engine Litigation II, 8:18-cv-02223 (C.D. Cal.), docs 79-2, 94-8, 163

The primary Hyundai release lists the models and engines. The settlement agreement defines the coverage as 15 years from original retail delivery or 150,000 miles, whichever comes first, covering short-block and long-block damage from connecting rod bearing failure. The final approval order contrasts this with Engine I's lifetime warranty, noting NHTSA's concern that lifetime warranties encourage owners to keep vehicles too long. Every 2015–2019 covered vehicle is still inside 15 years in 2026, but vehicles over 150k miles are not covered. Conditions: the KSDS software update must be done first, coverage excludes 'Exceptional Neglect', dealer-owned vehicles are excluded, and coverage depends on the engine, so it must be checked by VIN. CarPro, Repairer Driven News, ClassAction.org and Hagens Berman confirm the details.

### Under the Martinez v. Nissan class settlement, Nissan extended CVT warranty coverage (transmission assembly, valve body, torque converter and control unit) on the 2017–2018 Altima and the 2018–2019 Sentra, Versa and Versa Note from 60 months/60,000 miles to 84 months/84,000 miles. By September 2026 that coverage has expired or nearly expired for almost all of these cars.

- Confidence: high · Vote: 3-0 ([13],[14] merged)
- Sources: https://bbbprograms.org/programs/all-programs/class-action-arbitration-program/nissan-cvt_class-action, Nissan Campaign Bulletin 'CVT Warranty Extension' (NHTSA MC-10246457-0001, Rev. 1, Nov 2, 2023), altimasentraversacvtsettlement2022.com

The BBB page, which is the settlement's named arbitrator, states the 60/60 to 84/84 extension for each model. Nissan's dealer bulletin (activation date May 23, 2023) confirms the covered parts and says this is a warranty extension, not a safety recall. The case is Martinez v. Nissan North America, No. 3:22-cv-00354 (M.D. Tenn.). The 84-month clock runs from the original in-service date, so a 2017–2018 Altima sold before about September 2019 is out of coverage, and 2019 cars are at or near the end. Owners who opted out of the class never got the extension. For a buyer, these CVT model years carry transmission risk with no warranty backstop left.

Subaru says the extension is not a response to any specific defect. It is still in force as of September 2026.

- Sources: https://dot.report/bulletins/11019956, static.oemdtc.com/NHTSA-PDFs/MC-11019956-0001.pdf, Subaru TSB 16-155-25R (NHTSA MC-11021247; revision MC-11032037, Apr 24, 2026)

### Ford Customer Satisfaction Program 21N03 covered cam phaser noise on the 2nd-generation 3.5L EcoBoost: a rattle on cold start after the vehicle sits, or a knock at warm idle. It applied to 2017–2020 F-150s and was later widened to 2018–2020 Expedition/Navigator and the F-150 Raptor/Limited. Ford says the noise does not affect safety, performance or emissions. The program has expired, so a used buyer now pays for the repair.

- Confidence: high · Vote: 3-0 (claim [17]); a competing claim about expiry dates was refuted 0-3
- Sources: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2021/MC-10189762-0001.pdf, Ford 21N03 Supplement #4 (NHTSA ID 10209366, Mar 25, 2022)

Ford's program document (Mar 2, 2021) replaced all four phasers, fully covered to 69,999 miles and prorated down to 0% at 90k. Supplement #4 extended coverage to January 1, 2023, provided the 21B10 engine-computer reflash was done by Feb 28, 2022. A different set of dates (Sept 1, 2022 / Aug 31, 2021) was refuted in this run. Ford's 'noise only' position is disputed: owners and independent mechanics report that a loose locking pin can damage the engine, and 2025–2026 complaints to NHTSA describe power loss and repeat failures when old HL3Z-6C525 parts were used. Those complaints are unverified. Dealers quote about $3,000–$7,000 for four phasers. Confidence in the symptoms and Ford's position is high; the exact expiry date is medium.

### Ford TSB 23-2123 (April 13, 2023; later superseded by 23-2152) covers harsh engagement, harsh shifts or delayed shifts in the 10R80 10-speed automatic on 2017–2020 F-150, 2018–2021 Expedition/Navigator/Mustang and 2019–2023 Ranger. These can come with a check-engine light and shift-solenoid or gear-ratio trouble codes. The fix is a service bulletin repair, not a recall or warranty extension.

- Confidence: high · Vote: 3-0
- Sources: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2023/MC-10234596-0002.pdf, static.oemdtc.com/TSB/MC-10234596-0002.pdf, Ford TSB 23-2152 (NHTSA MC-10236683), Ford broader 10R60/10R80 TSB (NHTSA MC-10236680)

The TSB text lists the affected models and the trouble codes: P0751/P0752/P0756/P0757/P0761/P0762/P0766/P0767/P0771/P0772, P2700–P2705/P2707/P2708, P0729/P0731–P0736, P076F, P07D9, P07F6 and P07F7. The repair is an engine/transmission computer reflash with an adaptive relearn drive, or a main control valve body overhaul. Ford marks 23-2123 as superseded; 23-2152 revises the stated cause to keep-alive memory in the computer or the valve body. A broader bulletin covers 10R80 units built on or before Aug 15, 2022 and 10R60 units built on or before Dec 23, 2022, and later model-specific bulletins exist (25-2023, 24-2046). With no extension, most of these vehicles are past Ford's 5-year/60k powertrain warranty. Buyers should check shift quality and look for a record of the reflash or valve body work.

## Caveats

1) Several parts of the brief have no verified evidence. No claim survived about the GM 6.2L L87 engine recall (reported to cover 2021–2024 full-size GM trucks and SUVs with the 6.2L), Honda 1.5T oil dilution, GM AFM/DFM lifter failures, the vehicles covered by the 2019 Theta II GDI settlement, J.D. Power VDS results from 2018 to 2025, or iSeeCars' share-reaching-200k figures. Treat any statement about these as unverified, and check the VIN on NHTSA.gov. 2) The top picks for each segment are syntheses. The 2026 VDS awards are for the 2023 model year only and name models, not engines. Engine details (GX 460 V8, 4Runner 4.0 V6) come from the verifier or from which engines were offered that year. VDS counts three-year owner-reported problems, led by infotainment, and does not measure powertrain durability. 3) CR's Top Picks are value picks that change monthly. The September 2026 edition could not be loaded directly; its contents come from Exa's indexed highlights and a July 2026 copy. The model years on CR's 42-worst list come from secondary coverage plus CR's model pages, not from the paywalled list. 4) The iSeeCars evidence carries a 2-1 vote. It is based on 2022 odometer data drawn mostly from pre-2015 vehicles and works at the model level only. Claims about iSeeCars' 2025 study and its truck and sedan lifespan rankings were refuted 0-3, so pickup and sedan longevity comparisons are unverified. 5) Warranty status depends on timing. The Nissan CVT extensions have expired or nearly expired. Ford 21N03 has expired; one date set was refuted, and the verifier found January 1, 2023. The Subaru 10-year/100k extension is still in force, but its window for vehicles over 100k closed June 30, 2026. Hyundai/Kia Engine II coverage requires the KSDS update, applies only to rod-bearing damage and excludes vehicles over 150k miles. 6) The current V35A status (26V320, the inspection-only remedy for 25V767, the July 2024 improved-bearing cutoff, the August 2026 owner letters) comes partly from secondary sources. 7) Many primary documents were read through Exa or mirror sites because the proxy blocked direct fetches. 8) Some award winners fall outside the scope: the 2023 Sienna is a hybrid-only minivan and the 2023 Lexus UX was sold as a hybrid. The 2023+ Sequoia is hybrid-only, so the Sequoia longevity finding applies to the pre-2023 5.7L V8 models by inference. The 2022–23 F-150 Hybrid on CR's worst list is also out of scope.
