# 01 — Survey & Longevity Datasets (J.D. Power VDS, Consumer Reports, iSeeCars, RepairPal, others)

Research agent 1 of 7. Compiled 2026-09-26. Scope: the large survey/longevity datasets that other agents cross-reference for US used sedans/compacts, SUVs/crossovers and pickups, model years 2015–2025.

**Grading key:** A = primary (J.D. Power's own release/PDF/award page, CR's own article/press release, iSeeCars' own study page, NHTSA, OEM). B = 2+ independent quality outlets converging. C = single secondary source; RepairPal. D = forums/listicles/dealer blogs (leads only).

**Access notes:** jdpower.com is blocked by the session's egress proxy for WebFetch; J.D. Power pages were read through the Exa fetcher (same primary pages), so they stay grade A. The award winner images on the press-release pages aren't in the text, so "Top three per segment" lists come from J.D. Power's own press-release PDFs where Exa could read them (2020, 2021, 2024), from J.D. Power's consumer awards pages (jdpower.com/cars/ratings/dependability/YYYY), or from B-grade media coverage. ## 1. J.D. Power U.S. Vehicle Dependability Study (VDS), by study year

**How to read it:** study year N covers original owners of model year N−3 in their third year of ownership. PP100 = problems per 100 vehicles; lower is better. **The study was redesigned in 2022** (184 problem areas in 9 categories, adding driving assistance, versus 177 problems in 8 categories before). PP100 values from before and after 2022 are **not comparable**: the industry average jumped from 121 (2021) to 192 (2022) mostly because of the redesign.

Other 2026 VDS facts (A): infotainment remains the worst category at 56.7 PP100, then exterior at 27.5. The top problem for the third year running is Android Auto/CarPlay connectivity (8.9 PP100). Four of the top five problems are phone integration. Premium brands average 217 against mass market ~200 (a 17 PP100 gap). By powertrain: gas 198, hybrid 213, BEV 237, PHEV 281. From 2027 J.D. Power adds year-round data collection and "integrated verified repair data" (starting April 2026). That is a methodology break to flag for the 2027 study.

2025 VDS (A): 27 all-new 2022 models averaged 241 PP100 against 196 for carryover models, and only 4 of the 27 beat their segment average. That supports the "avoid first-year models" rule. Hybrids had the fewest problems (199), then gas (200), BEV (223), diesel (233) and PHEV (242).

2024 VDS (A): problems grew 17% between 90 days and three years of ownership (the rate was 12% in 2023). By powertrain: BEV 256, PHEV 216, hybrid 191, gas 187.

## 3. Consumer Reports brand reliability rankings by year (new-car predicted reliability)

**How to read it:** CR's annual survey asks members about problems in the past 12 months across 17 trouble areas for gas cars, 19 for hybrids, 20 for PHEVs and up to 12 for EVs, weighted by severity. It gives each new model a **predicted** reliability score (0/1–100) from the most recent ~3 model years, and ranks brands on the average of their models. Brands need at least 2 models with enough data. Survey sizes: ~740k vehicles (2015) → ~640k (2017) → 500k+ (2018) → 420k (2019) → 300k+ (2020–22) → 330k (2023) → 300k+ (2024) → ~380k (2025). The years in the headers are **release years**, usually Oct–Dec, and cover the next model year's predictions.

Full score tables are **coverage of CR data**: Auto123, Cars.com, KBB, Carscoops, Motor1, Fox Business, Motor Illustrated, Visual Capitalist, Driven. ## 5. iSeeCars longevity studies

**Method (A, iSeeCars):** iSeeCars computes the average odometer reading of each model at each vehicle age from ~400 million used-car records and fits a proprietary survival model that estimates the probability of reaching a mileage threshold. Heavy-duty trucks are excluded from the main list but included in the truck list. Results are pooled across **all generations and model years** of a nameplate. That is a major caveat for model-year buyers: the Sequoia and Tundra scores are dominated by pre-2022 V8 trucks. iSeeCars' own caveat: "the reduced likelihood of a luxury car hitting 250,000 miles is more a reflection on how their owners use them… versus… how well they are… built."

## 8. Methodology notes and caveats

| | **J.D. Power VDS** | **Consumer Reports** | **iSeeCars longevity** | **RepairPal** | **CarMD** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit measured | Owner-reported problems in the **past 12 months** of **year 3** (PP100) | Owner-reported problems in the past 12 months, **severity-weighted**, scored **relative to the average for the same model year** | Estimated probability that a model reaches 250k miles (from odometer-by-age survival modeling) | Shop repair orders: cost, frequency, severity | Check-engine-light diagnoses |
| Who | **Original owners**, J.D. Power sample (~30–37k per year) | CR members (volunteers; more educated and affluent than average). ~300–380k vehicles per year; **~200–300 samples per model-year** | ~395–400M vehicle records from listings/registrations | Repair orders from 2,000+ network shops | CarMD/Innova device users |
| Problem areas | 177 in 8 categories (≤2021); **184 in 9 categories (2022+)** | 17 (ICE), 19 (hybrid), 20 (PHEV), ≤12 (EV) | n/a | n/a | OBD-II codes |
| Severity weighting | **None.** A CarPlay dropout counts the same as a transmission failure | **Yes.** Engine, transmission and EV battery weighted more | n/a | Yes (a "severe" bucket) | n/a |
| Model-year granularity | One MY per study | Every MY (used verdicts); new-car prediction = last ~3 MYs of the same generation | **None.** Pooled across all MYs and generations | Pooled MY2010–2019 | By MY (2020 edition) |
| Main biases | Counts **design/usability complaints and infotainment** as "problems." Many phone-integration issues aren't mechanical. Premium brands carry more tech and so more complaints. Tesla is unranked (states require permission). **J.D. Power is funded by automakers**: consulting, data access, and ad-licensing fees for award claims | Self-selected members, with possible feedback loops. **Shrinking sample** (about 10% fewer responses in 2024 per a critic, C). Brands with few CR-member owners go unranked (Chrysler, Dodge, Ram, Mercedes, Porsche and others in 2024). Brand score = unweighted average of models | Measures **use intensity and ownership duration plus survival**, not failures. Fleet and work usage inflates HD trucks and Suburbans. Luxury cars are driven less. Odometer-fraud and single-reading limits. Pools old generations | Cost-driven; penalizes trucks and luxury; stale vintage | Self-selected; engine and emissions only; implausible brand results |
| Time horizon | Year 3 only (just as the 3/36 warranty ends) | Every year of age; the CR used-brand ranking covers 5–10-year-old cars | Lifetime | Lifetime average | Recent calendar year |

**What each is good for, for a used buyer of MY2015–2025:**
1. **CR model-year verdicts and "avoid" lists** are the best available **model-year-specific, severity-weighted, multi-year-of-age** signal. They are the primary tool for "which years to avoid."
2. **J.D. Power VDS awards** are a good **3-year snapshot of a specific MY**, with large random samples of original owners. They overweight tech annoyances and underweight catastrophic failures, and they say nothing about years 4–10.
3. **iSeeCars** is a good **durability-at-high-mileage** signal for a nameplate's historical generations. It isn't MY-specific and doesn't transfer to redesigned generations.
4. **RepairPal and CarMD** are ownership-cost context only.

**Scale caveat:** J.D. Power itself notes that "rankings are based on numerical scores, and not necessarily on statistical significance." Adjacent brands separated by a few PP100 are ties. CR says a score within roughly ±20% of average should be read as "about average" (per a TTAC critique, C). Treat mid-table ranks in both systems as noise; only the extremes are informative.

## 10. Falsification pass log

**Q1. Is J.D. Power VDS a valid "reliability" measure?**
Criticism found:
- Autoblog opinion (2016): VDS ranked Subaru and Mazda near the bottom "because they don't have very good infotainment systems," while engine and transmission problems were falling.
- Autoweek (2017): J.D. Power counts operator error as a quality problem, and J.D. Power's SVP defended that as a "quality of service" issue.
- Car and Driver (2011): IQS "problems" include design dislikes.
- Driving.ca (2025): VDS "weighs non-critical problems such as smartphone connectivity equally with critical mechanical issues." J.D. Power is "largely funded by automakers" and collects ad-licensing fees.
- TTAC (2021): conflict-of-interest argument.
- Together these make a **B-grade critique**.

J.D. Power's own data confirms it:
- Infotainment is the #1 category (56.7 PP100 in 2026), and 4 of the top 5 problems are phone integration (A).
- J.D. Power doesn't claim statistical significance between adjacent ranks (A).

Verdict: VDS survives as a **year-3 owner-experience** measure with a large random sample of original owners. It is **not** a mechanical-durability measure. Weigh it below CR for "will this break," and use it mainly to confirm or deny specific MYs.

**Q2. Is CR's survey biased?**
Criticism found:
- Self-selected members, a possible feedback loop, and undisclosed variances (Stellpower/Allpar, D/C).
- Shrinking sample: about 10% fewer responses in 2024, several brands unranked, Cadillac ranked on 2 of 7 models (Automotive Addicts, C).
- Predictions lag real changes (Austin Statesman, 2012, C; TTAC 2010, C).

CR's own FAQ (A) acknowledges several points:
- Members are "more educated and affluent than the general population."
- Samples are ~200–300 per model-year.
- CR went online-only in 2015, which "shrank our respondent pool."
- CR argues that factual problem questions are insensitive to sample demographics.

Verdict: CR is the best **MY-level, severity-weighted** source, but thin samples make single-MY verdicts noisy. It is **weaker for brands and models CR members rarely own**: Ram, Dodge, Chrysler and HD trucks were often unranked in 2024–2025. For those segments, lean more on VDS, NHTSA complaints and recalls (other agents), and iSeeCars.

**Q3. Is iSeeCars "longevity" really reliability?**
- iSeeCars itself says luxury results reflect usage, not build quality (A).
- TFLcar (Sep 2026, C): the scores are historical, and recent redesigns (twin-turbo V6 Tundra/Sequoia, turbo-4 Tacoma/4Runner, new RX and GX) aren't represented.
- Morning Overview (D): registration/fleet retention and odometer-fraud caveats.

Verdict: treat iSeeCars as **nameplate-historical durability plus usage intensity**. It is **not transferable to 2022+ redesigns** of the Tundra, Sequoia, Tacoma, 4Runner, RX or GX, which is exactly where CR shows teething problems (2022–2023 Tundra on the CR avoid list; the Tacoma below average in CR Dec 2024 before improving).

**Q5. Does the pandemic era distort MY2021–2023?**
Yes (A):
- J.D. Power's 2024 and 2025 VDS explicitly blame "the tumultuous time during which these vehicles were built."
- The industry average rose from 186 (MY20) to 190 (MY21), 202 (MY22) and 204 (MY23).
- New 2022 models averaged 241 PP100 against 196 for carryovers.
- Implication: MY2021–2023 used vehicles carry above-baseline risk. Prefer carryover (non-redesigned) models from those years.

**Q6. Methodology breaks to flag:**
- VDS changed in 2022, so PP100 isn't comparable across the break.
- VDS will change again in 2027 (adding repair data and year-round collection).
- CR added EV and hybrid trouble areas in 2023 (17 to 20).
- CR's brand ranking uses different brand sets each year (22–30 brands).
- iSeeCars' average rose from 4.8% to 5.4% in 2026, which changes the "x times average" multiples.

**Overall weighting recommendation for the report:**
1. **Primary:** CR model-year reliability verdicts and "avoid" lists (A/C), for MY-specific decisions.
2. **Strong corroboration:** J.D. Power VDS segment awards and top-3 lists for that exact MY (A). Treat them as positive evidence when CR agrees, and flag as "contested" when CR disagrees (§9.2).
3. **Durability tiebreaker:** iSeeCars 250k probabilities (A/B), only for generations the data actually covers (mostly pre-2022).
4. **Context only:** RepairPal (C) for cost; CarMD (C) is essentially obsolete for model rankings.
5. Don't use single-year brand ranks to override model-year data. Brand ranks swing 5–10 places between years when there are few models or a big redesign.
